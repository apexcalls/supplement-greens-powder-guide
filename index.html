Greens Powders: What the Testing Standards Actually Require

Greens powders are food supplements, not medicines, and the testing standards that apply to them differ from what many buyers assume. Medicines go through pre-market approval: regulators review safety and efficacy data before a single bottle is sold. Supplements go through none of that. A greens powder can legally reach shelves without any clinical testing of the product itself.

That does not mean all greens powders are untested. The serious brands invest in third-party laboratory testing for each batch — checking identity of the botanical ingredients, screening for heavy metals and microbial contamination, and verifying that nutrient levels on the label match what is in the bag. The difference from medicines is that this testing is voluntary, not mandatory.

So the question to ask is not "is it tested like a drug?" but "who tested THIS batch, and where is the certificate?" A certificate of analysis names the lab, the batch number, and the results. Brands that publish COAs give you something verifiable; brands that only say "quality tested" give you a slogan.
